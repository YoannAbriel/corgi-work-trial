## 2026-09-08T08:04:03+00:00 | Explicit user decision | Money rules decided by Yoann, superseding the assistant proposals of 07:39

Common example: policy premium $1,200 written March 1, 2028, term to March 1, 2029 (366 days, 2028 is a leap year), cancelled after 100 days. Exact earned premium = 120000 x 100 / 366 = 32786.885 cents.

1. Rounding penny: the insurer eats it. Earned premium is floored (32786 cents), the customer refund is ceiled (87214 cents). Always in the customer's favor, one rule, deterministic. Rejected: customer eats the penny; round to nearest.
2. Term end: same calendar date next year, or the last day of February when that date does not exist. A policy written February 29, 2028 ends February 28, 2029; one written March 1, 2028 ends March 1, 2029 (366 days). Term days are always the actual count. Rejected: overflow to March 1.
3. Approval thresholds (assumptions of this build, not Corgi rules): money-out (claim payments and refunds) above $1,000 requires a distinct human approver, never the initiator and never an agent; an endorsement adding more than $500 of premium requires customer approval. Rejected: $500 / $250; approval on every amount.
4. Taxes and fees on cancellation: the state premium tax follows the premium and is refunded on the unearned portion; the flat policy fee is fully earned at issuance and never refunded. Example with 3% tax and a $25 fee on a $1,261 charge: refund = 87214 + 2617 + 0 = 89831 cents ($898.31), the tax refund being ceiled in the customer's favor. Rejected: fee also pro-rata; nothing refunded but premium.

Unchanged and already Yoann's decisions: commission earned on collected premium with clawback on refund; pro-rata cancellation with short-rate representable. The modeled state and its tax rate will be chosen from an official source at implementation and put to Yoann before being recorded.

## 2026-09-08T09:29:25+00:00 | Explicit user decision | Modeled state: California, premium tax 2.35 percent (235 bps), from official sources

Proposed by the B2 delegate's research (all sources accessed 2026-09-08) and decided by Yoann. Primary source: California Constitution, Article XIII, Section 28(d): "The rate of the tax to be applied to the basis of the annual tax in respect to each year is 2.35 percent." (https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CONS&sectionNum=SEC.%2028.&article=XIII). Corroborated by Revenue and Taxation Code section 12202 (2.35 percent for every year except 1982 to 1985 at 2.33 percent) and the California Department of Insurance FS-001 tax return instructions for 2025 ("Line 2: Tax Rate - the tax rate of 2.35 %"). Effective date stored as 1986-01-01, labeled derived from the RTC 12202 exception window, not stated verbatim by the source. Scope: admitted property and casualty premium, which is our commercial liability product; surplus lines (3 percent, Ins. Code 1775.5) are out of scope. Tax base per Constitution art. XIII s. 28(c): gross premiums received less return premiums, which matches our collected basis and pro-rata tax refund on cancellation.

Interpretation recorded, not a legal claim: the tax is legally owed by the insurer on its premiums; the brief asks that the customer's charge include state premium tax and flat fees as separate pieces that never pollute earned premium, so the application shows a "state premium tax" line at 2.35 percent on the premium, owed to the state, and refunds it pro-rata with the premium. The flat policy fee ($25) remains an assumption of this build, fully earned at issuance.

Recited example updated accordingly: premium $1,200 written March 1, 2028 (365 days), California tax 2.35% = $28.20, fee $25, charge $1,253.20; cancelled on day 100: unearned 87124 cents, tax refunded ceil(87124 x 2.35%) = 2048 cents, fee 0, total refund 89172 cents ($891.72). Commission 15% on the $1,200 collected: $180.

## 2026-09-08T16:09:04+00:00 | Explicit user decision | Cancelling an endorsed policy refunds segment by segment

Each endorsement is a written premium segment with its own effective date and its own remaining days: the issuance segment earns over the whole term, an endorsement segment earns from its effective date over the days that remained then. At cancellation each segment's earned part is computed and rounded on its own (floor on earned, so the unearned part rounds in the customer's favour), and the refund is the sum of the unearned parts, which ties cent for cent to the premium_written entries in the journal. Consequence, stated: a cent or two more is refunded than if the cover in force were priced as one line over the remaining days. Example: $1,200 written 2028-03-01, raised by $600 annual effective 2028-06-09 (43561 cents written for the segment), cancelled 2028-09-01: earned 60493 + 13808 = 74301, unearned 89260, tax back 2098, clawback 13389. Yoann's words: "c'est une question de dates qui ne sont pas au même moment ... l'option A me semble plus logique". Closes review item F-B4-03.
